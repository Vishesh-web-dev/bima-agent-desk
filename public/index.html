National Insurance agent · Ahmedabad · Nov 2026

Finding the first 50 customers

The licence lets him sell National Insurance's general insurance: vehicles, health, shops, homes and travel. Customers come from three places: people who already know him, partners who meet people at the moment they need cover, and people searching for an agent nearby. Every policy renews each year, so each customer he wins pays him again next year.

The short version

  1. Start with the 100 people who already trust him. Ask for their renewal dates, not for a sale.
  2. Lead with health cover for parents and car policies. They pay the most commission per policy.
  3. Build 10 partners (garages, used-car dealers, visa consultants, CAs) who meet people right when they need insurance.
  4. Log every expiry date in the Clients tab and message 30 days before. Renewals are where the income adds up.

What he can sell

National Insurance Company Ltd. is a Government of India general insurer. Everything here comes under his licence.

Motor

Car, two-wheeler and commercial vehicle (taxi, tempo, truck, school van). Third-party cover is compulsory by law, so every vehicle renews every year.

Health

Family floater, senior citizen, top-up and super top-up mediclaim (National Parivar Mediclaim, National Mediclaim Plus, Varistha Mediclaim and others).

Shop & business

Shopkeeper's policy (fire, burglary, cash), fire policies for godowns and small factories, and other business cover.

Home

House building and contents against fire, flood, earthquake and burglary. New flat buyers are the easiest ask.

Travel & accident

Overseas mediclaim for students and travellers, and personal accident cover for anyone who rides or drives daily.

Not with this licence: life insurance (term plans, LIC-type plans). IRDAI's agent rules let one person hold one life, one general and one standalone-health agency at the same time, so he can add a life insurer later and sell more to the same customers. Confirm with the branch before applying.

Where customers come from

Ranked by how quickly each usually turns into a first policy.

  1. Family, friends, neighbours, colleagues

    They already trust him. Every adult with a vehicle or with parents is a prospect, and each one knows ten more.

    First moveWrite down 100 names. Send the "Tell everyone" message to 15–20 a day, one by one. Save every renewal date in Clients, even if it's months away.

  2. Health cover for parents over 55

    Individual health premiums have had no GST since 22 Sep 2025. Since 1 Apr 2024 there's no upper age limit for buying a new policy, and the pre-existing-disease wait is at most 3 years. Children of older parents worry about hospital bills.

    First moveAsk every contact: "Are your parents covered, and for how much?"

  3. Car and bike renewals

    Many people renew online in minutes. He wins on service: the right IDV, keeping the no-claim bonus, the right add-ons, and help when there's a claim.

    First moveOffer a free policy check-up: "Send me your policy PDF and I'll tell you if you're under-insured or overpaying."

  4. Partners who meet people at the right moment

    Garages and body shops (accident claims), used-car dealers and RTO agents (a sold vehicle needs insurance transferred), visa and overseas-education consultants (student and travel mediclaim), CAs and tax consultants (business clients need shop and fire cover), property brokers and builders (new home, home insurance).

    First moveVisit 2 partners a week with the partner message. Repay them with referrals and customers, never with a cut of commission.

  5. Shops and small businesses

    Lots of shops have no cover, or cover far below their stock value. One fire or break-in can wipe out a small trader.

    First movePick one market and visit 10 shops a week. Start where people already know the family.

  6. Commercial vehicle owners

    Transporters, school-van operators, tempo and taxi owners. One fleet owner can mean 5–20 policies renewing every year.

    First moveAsk partner garages and RTO agents to introduce him to fleet owners.

  7. Google and WhatsApp Business

    People search "insurance agent near me". A free Google Business Profile with a few reviews shows up there. WhatsApp Business adds a catalogue, labels and status updates.

    First moveSet up both in week 1 using the profile text in Messages. Ask every happy customer for a Google review.

  8. Housing society and community

    A free "policy check" desk on a Sunday at a society, community event or office. People bring their policy papers and questions.

    First moveAsk one society secretary for permission. Aim for one desk a month.

Rules that protect the licence

  • Never give a discount out of his commission.Section 41 of the Insurance Act bans rebates. The penalty goes up to ₹10 lakh, and the licence can be cancelled. Compete on service instead.
  • Don't pay referrers a share of commission.Only licensed people can be paid for insurance business. Repay partners with referrals.
  • Public ads need National Insurance's written approval.Posters, Instagram or Facebook posts and reels about products must be approved by the insurer first. The insurer's own creatives and a plain name, phone and licence card don't need it. Ask the Development Officer for approved material.
  • Fill proposal forms truthfully.Hiding an illness or a past claim to get a lower premium leads to a rejected claim, and that customer tells everyone.
  • Message people he knows, not strangers in bulk.Bulk WhatsApp gets the number banned, and cold calls to DND numbers break TRAI rules. Ask before saving someone's details.
